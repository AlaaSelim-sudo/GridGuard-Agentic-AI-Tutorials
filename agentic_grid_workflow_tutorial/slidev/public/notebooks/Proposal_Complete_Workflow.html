P2 — Voltage support with OpenDSS: from a policy proposal to verified refinement

The fleet command is $u=[a,b]$: $a$ is active-power participation and $b$ is reactive-power participation, both relative to inverter kVA. Inverter $i$ receives $P_i=aS_i$ and $Q_i=bS_i$, where $S_i$ is its nameplate rating. The limits include $0\leq a\leq1$, $|b|\leq0.44$, and $a^2+b^2\leq1$.

The current snapshot cost is

$$J(a,b)=(1-a)^2+0.05b^2+10\,\mathrm{mean}_j[(V_j-1.01)^2]+0.05\|[a,b]-u_{\mathrm{previous}}\|_2^2.$$

Here $V_j$ is a modeled phase-voltage magnitude in per unit, $j$ indexes the evaluated phase voltages, and $u_{\mathrm{previous}}=[0.25,0]$. A smaller cost is preferred only after voltage, current, capability and evidence checks pass. The current allowance is 80% of each modeled line rating. The MPC uses forecast load multipliers $[1,0.94,1.06]$, a three-step horizon and discount 0.98; its horizon objective is distinct from the displayed snapshot ranking cost.

The planning tools provide a saved SAC action and a conservative seed. The refinement menu can request a fresh MPC first action or a small local change. OpenDSS evaluates the supplied unbalanced MV/LV feeder. Read the exact Llama choice, the next simulation, and the gate's response together. A persuasive reason cannot rescue a rejected current constraint.

P3 — Repeat voltage support with three-phase LinDistFlow

The Python linear model retains coupled phase impedances and evaluates MV primary phase voltages and currents. It aggregates LV devices through ideal service transformers. It omits internal LV voltage drops, LV line-current constraints and transformer thermal checks. Consequently, a pass in this model does not certify the complete OpenDSS feeder.

For a radial branch $i\rightarrow j$, the reduced model balances downstream complex power and approximates squared-voltage drop:

$$S_{ij}=s_j+\sum_{k:j\rightarrow k} S_{jk},\qquad w_j=w_i-2\,\operatorname{Re}\!\left[\overline{\beta}\odot\bigl(Z_{ij}(\beta\odot\overline{S_{ij}})\bigr)\right].$$

$S_{ij}$ and $s_j$ are three-phase complex powers in VA; $w=|V|^2$ is in V²; $Z_{ij}$ is the phase-impedance matrix in ohms; $\beta=[1,e^{-j2\pi/3},e^{-j4\pi/3}]^\top$ supplies nominal phase references; $\odot$ is elementwise multiplication. This is a declared approximation, not an error bound.

The same SAC weights, objective definition, search menu and maximum budget are used. Numerical values can differ because both model approximation and constraint coverage differ. The optional saved Qwen route illustrates model portability on one case; it does not establish a model leaderboard.

P4 — Restoration with OpenDSS: improve service without changing policy

Awareness establishes a fault-isolated feeder, a permitted 0.25 km short bypass rated at 100 A, a forbidden 2 km long bypass, and unavailable PV. The candidate variable $f$ disconnects a specified fraction of the ordered noncritical load objects. It is not a fraction of total kW. Every tenth sorted load object defines an artificial critical-priority class for reproducibility.

The model-based catalog ranks feasible actions by

$$\min_f J_R(f)=-\left(P_{\mathrm{served}}(f)+9P_{\mathrm{critical,served}}(f)\right).$$

Both powers are sums of nominal connected load kW. Thus a critical kW has total weight 10. The gate checks voltages, thermal ratings, MV connectivity/radiality, the isolated fault, the permitted short tie, PV-off policy, and full service of the modeled critical set. The two initial fractions are $f=0.50$ and $0.90$; permitted refinement trials are $0.65$ and $0.75$.

This is a bounded feeder action search, not a full-feeder mixed-integer optimization. The separate six-node MILP lesson explains standard binary pickup/energization and connectivity formulations. These feeder snapshots do not validate physical switching order, relay coordination, field interlocks or crew safety.

P6 — Two cyber cases: isolated corruption and stealth $Hc$

The separate four-bus DC fixture has a state $x\in\mathbb{R}^3$ of non-slack voltage angles. The original measurement matrix $H\in\mathbb{R}^{8\times3}$ maps those angles to four flows, three injections and one angle measurement. With measurement vector $z$ and diagonal noise covariance $R$, weighted least squares solves

$$\hat{x}=\arg\min_x (z-Hx)^\top R^{-1}(z-Hx),\qquad T=(z-H\hat{x})^\top R^{-1}(z-H\hat{x}).$$

The residual test compares $T$ with a chi-square threshold under the stated Gaussian model and five residual degrees of freedom. One case corrupts a single flow measurement. The other adds $a=Hc$: the shifted estimate $\hat{x}+c$ can leave the residual unchanged, so a small residual is not proof of trustworthy data.

The recovery demonstration assumes three additional protected angle measurements with $H_p=I_3$. They are not a subset of the original eight readings, and authentication is not implemented. Their exactly determined estimate has zero residual degrees of freedom. The workflow checks agreement with these assumed trusted channels and requires their provenance before recommending redispatch.

The model-based control minimizes $\tfrac12u^2$, subject to $0\leq u\leq0.5$ and

$$-\ell+\delta\leq F\hat{x}+F B^{-1}d\,u\leq\ell-\delta.$$

$u$ is flexible injection in per unit, $F$ maps angles to line flows, $B$ is the reduced susceptance matrix, $d$ selects the flexible bus, $\ell$ is the vector of line-flow limits, and $\delta=0.01$ pu is the planning margin. The simulator also reports known toy truth only as an offline audit; the operational gate does not use that oracle.
