One notebook, three cases, 45 minutes of hands-on work inside a three-hour lecture.

The learning objective is to build a checked decision workflow: understand a request, choose a numerical tool, simulate a candidate, verify evidence, refine within a budget, and request an operator readback. A fluent answer is not electrical evidence.

L2 · Model-based planning and model-free action proposals

Predict: will a fast saved policy necessarily remain feasible after a current limit changes?

Let $u=[a,b]$, where $a$ is active-power participation and $b$ is reactive-power participation across the supplied inverter fleet: $P_i=aS_i,\ Q_i=bS_i$. The capability constraint is $a^2+b^2\leq1$, with $0\leq a\leq1,\ |b|\leq0.44$. Both controls are continuous.

MPC solves a three-step local sensitivity optimization, minimizing \[ \sum_{k=0}^{2}\gamma^k\left[(1-a_k)^2+0.05b_k^2+ 10\operatorname{mean}(V_k-1.01)^2+0.05\lVert u_k-u_{k-1}\rVert^2\right], \] subject to modeled voltage/current limits and inverter capability. $V_k$ contains per-unit phase voltages; $\gamma=0.98$ discounts later cost. Only the first action is evaluated in the comparison table. This is local feasible planning, without a global optimum or stability certificate.

SAC is a model-free continuous policy learned previously from simulator experience. The saved NumPy actor performs new inference here. Its training conditions differ from this derated test, so this is a transfer check. PPO is another continuous policy-learning method, but no PPO policy is trained in this tutorial.

Do: run the default, then change LOAD_SCALE to 1.05 or RATING_FRACTION to 0.75. Compare feasibility before comparing cost. A rejected action has no eligible cost.

Sources: Rawlings, Mayne & Diehl, MPC; Haarnoja et al., SAC; Gan & Low, multiphase linear models.

L4 · Learned response selection is a trial budget decision

The response selector is a contextual bandit, a one-step RL problem. For context $x$, canonical operation $o$, and feature vector $\phi(x,o)$, training uses \[ \hat\theta=A^{-1}b,\quad o_t=\arg\max_o\left[\hat\theta^T\phi(x,o)+\alpha\sqrt{\phi(x,o)^TA^{-1}\phi(x,o)}\right], \] \[ A\leftarrow A+\phi_t\phi_t^T,\qquad b\leftarrow b+r_t\phi_t. \]

$A$ is a regularized feature covariance, $b$ accumulates selected rewards, and $\alpha$ controls exploration. The frozen test policy omits the exploration bonus and makes no updates. The reward concerns a selected trial's verified improvement with a trial penalty. It does not score persuasive prose or decide which LLM to call: all three calls have already occurred.

Do: reproduce the frozen choice, then run a fresh model check of the original selected candidate. This is replay plus new verification, not a new LLM decision. In the archived 12-case test, learned selection ties voting; the fixed reactive-relaxation diagnostic matches both. Do not infer RL superiority from this cell.

Source: Li et al., contextual bandits.

L6 · Restoration: the objective expresses a service policy

Predict: does maximizing total restored kW also maximize critical-load service?

On a six-node synthetic graph, choose binary pickup $x_i$ and energized edges $y_e$: \[ \max\sum_i w_i d_i x_i, \quad x_i\leq z_i, \quad -\bar P_e y_e\leq P_e\leq\bar P_e y_e. \]

$d_i$ is demand in kW, $w_i=10$ for two critical loads and $1$ otherwise, and $z_i$ is node energization. Signed active-power balance supplies picked-up demand. Commodity flow connects energized nodes to the source; a radial edge-count constraint then enforces a tree. Faulted edges are unavailable. A second lexicographic solve preserves service and minimizes energized branches; this is not the number of switching operations from a prior state.

The baseline maximizes unweighted total kW on the same graph and constraints. Both are conventional numerical optimizers; any difference here is caused by the objective, not by Llama. Independent exhaustive enumeration checks the priority MILP optimum. The agentic task is to preserve the operator's priority contract when selecting and explaining the solver.

Do: sweep the tie capacity, then add 100. or 180. to the list. This toy active-power transport model omits AC voltage/reactive constraints and protection sequencing; it is not the supplied Greensboro switching system.

Source: Chen et al., restoration optimization.

L8 · Cyber: residual consistency is not truth

Predict: will a larger attack always produce a larger residual alarm?

Weighted least squares uses $z=Hx+\epsilon$, $R=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_i^2)$, \[ \hat x=(H^TR^{-1}H)^{-1}H^TR^{-1}z,\qquad J=(z-H\hat x)^TR^{-1}(z-H\hat x). \]

$x$ contains three non-slack DC voltage angles in radians; $z$ contains flows, injections and one angle; $H$ maps state to measurement. With independent Gaussian errors and a correct full-rank model, compare $J$ with a chi-square threshold using $m-n$ degrees of freedom and significance $0.01$.

An isolated bias creates inconsistency. The negative control $a=Hc$ shifts the estimated state by $c$ while leaving the residual unchanged. This attack assumes knowledge of $H$ and control of all modeled unprotected channels. Offline state error uses known simulation truth for teaching, not an online detector input.

Do: increase the scale range. Compare the two panels before explaining the alarm. Sources: Schweppe & Wildes, state estimation; Liu et al., false-data injection.

L9 · Trusted state, constrained response, explicit HOLD

Three additional angle channels are assumed independently trustworthy. This example does not implement authentication. Their WLS system is exactly determined, so it has zero residual degrees of freedom and makes no chi-square detection claim.

The model-based response solves $\min_{0\leq u\leq0.5}\tfrac12u^2$, subject to \[ |F\hat x+FB^{-1}e_2u|\leq\bar f-0.01. \]

$u$ is a flexible injection in per unit, balanced by the slack; $B$ is the reduced nodal susceptance matrix, $F$ maps angles to branch flows, $e_2$ selects the controlled node, and $\bar f$ is the branch-flow limit. The 0.01 p.u. margin is explicit. Known truth is used only for the offline outcome check. The gate may HOLD even when the numerical solver could return an action.
